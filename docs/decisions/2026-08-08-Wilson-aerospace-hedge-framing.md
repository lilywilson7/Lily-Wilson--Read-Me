## Executive Summary (≤150 words)

Our firm has a $20.0 million-equivalent EUR receivable due in one year, creating material uncertainty in the USD value of the cash we ultimately collect. At the assigned indicative one-year EUR/USD forward rate of 1.0935, the exposure represents approximately $21.87 million in USD proceeds. If EUR/USD falls to 1.00 by settlement, the same euro receivable would produce approximately $20.0 million, a potential $1.87 million reduction versus the forward-rate benchmark. Given the size of this exposure, hedging merits formal analysis before settlement. I recommend that we approve the next stages of the project to compare a forward, money-market hedge, and EUR put option on a consistent basis before selecting a hedge.

---

## Background & Objectives

The business risk is straightforward: the foreign-currency amount of the receivable is fixed, but its USD value can change with EUR/USD. A weaker euro reduces the dollars received and can create a meaningful budget or cash-flow shortfall. A forward hedge would lock in the exchange rate and provide certainty, but it would also eliminate upside if the euro strengthens. A money-market hedge can create similar certainty by borrowing euros, converting them to dollars, and investing the dollars, but it uses borrowing capacity and involves more moving parts. A EUR put option provides a floor under USD proceeds while preserving upside if the euro strengthens, but the option premium is paid whether or not the protection is ultimately needed.

## Methods

The analysis will be developed in four stages. Stage 2 will specify the workbook structure, inputs, named ranges, formulas, assumptions, and validation checks before construction. Stage 3 will use AI to assist with the workbook build, followed by a manual audit and correction of its calculations and logic. Stage 4 will replace placeholder assumptions with current, sourced, timestamped market data, including spot rates, interest rates, forward inputs, and option assumptions. Stage 5 will independently validate the results, verify key calculations by hand, compare the hedge alternatives, and produce an evidence-based recommendation.

---

## Limitations & Next Steps

The 1.0935 forward rate is an indicative scenario input rather than a live market quote, so it should not be treated as a final executable price. Option and money-market economics also depend on market inputs that will be established in later stages. I recommend approval to proceed with Stages 2–5 so management can evaluate cost, protection, and upside trade-offs before committing to a hedge.
